Session partial Local run

Stopped by the operator at the user's request after 3 h 56 min, in the task loop, with no playable game: 2 of 14 task cards done (T001 and T002a, each accepted at first review); of the 12 planned tasks, T002 was split into T002a and T002b, T002b blocked after three attempts, T003 interrupted and 9 to do. 17 sessions. The run stalled on replies that reasoned up to the 16,384-token output limit and wrote nothing: 16 of 182 replies were cut there, all reasoning with no content or tool call, and 7 of 9 builder sessions ended on one. A 2 GiB prompt cache, too small for this model, kept the cache share at 56% and the median time to first token at 27 s.

Measurements ↓

What was checked

  • Recorded as partial, not failed: bootstrap finished and two tasks were built and accepted before the stop, as in other runs stopped by the operator mid-loop with some tasks done. The runner state is paused in LOOP, not blocked; the run could be resumed but will not be.
  • Bootstrap took 4 sessions: spec and map (001) and verify (002) on their first attempt; the task-card step failed once (003 reasoned to the output limit and wrote no card) and was accepted on the second (004): 12 tasks and 34 requirements.
  • T001 was built in one session (005) and accepted by review (006). T002 failed three attempts (007 to 009): each session wrote no file and had no passing T002 check, so Tenaz rejected it (./verify --task exited OK but ran 0 checks of the task's own, and a task needs at least one). Session 010 split it into T002a and T002b. T002a was built (011) and accepted (012). T002b failed three attempts the same way (013 to 015); the second split (016) reasoned to the output limit and added nothing, and Tenaz blocked T002b. Session 017 started T003 and was stopped by the operator after 3 turns.
  • Cut replies: 16 replies reached the 16,384-token limit, and every one was all reasoning (reasoning tokens equal output tokens). TensorFold logged the matching warning 16 times. Each of the six builder sessions on T002 and T002b made two cut replies, about 33,000 output tokens per session, and wrote nothing. After a cut reply Tenaz lowered the reasoning effort to low for the next session, and TensorFold logged effort=low on 82 requests; the replies still ran to the limit.
  • Operator's diagnosis, from the private reasoning traces: most cut reasoning was deliberation, not repetition (in 5 of 7 cut builder sessions no paragraph repeats more than 10 times), with two loops at temperature 0 (008 and 013). The T002b card, written by the split before T002a was built, places the spawn on a corridor the grid T002a built does not have, and the model spent its budget reconciling the two.
  • Prompt cache: the server was started with --prompt-cache-gib 2; the October TensorFold runs used 6. TensorFold refused to keep a cache state 323 times ('kept nothing' warnings) because it did not fit the 2,048 MiB budget. Across the run 31 of 182 requests had no cached tokens. This was an operator set-up error, not a property of the engine.
  • Engine metrics: all 182 proxy records carry engine timings and match TensorFold 'done' summaries one-to-one, in order, by prompt, cached and output tokens; forward and backward matching agree, and TTFT, decode time, effort and draft counts agree. The 6 earlier summaries are Tenaz's preflight and are excluded, as is one 'client-left' entry at the operator's stop.
  • Memory: Tenaz's samples show pressure normal throughout, 0.9 GiB swapped in, and TensorFold's footprint at 45 GiB, 47 GiB at most. An orphan watcher found no leftover vite preview servers at any of its seven 30-minute checks.
  • Not built or played: the project holds only the scaffold and the work of T001 and T002a.
  • The prompt is the author's and is not republished; the project, raw logs, journal and sessions stay in the ignored local archive.

Limits of this evidence

Not a controlled comparison with the Sushi runs of the same workload: it changes the engine, the model (27B dense instead of Flash Next) and the Tenaz build. It was the first dsh run on TensorFold. Two things slowed it: replies that reasoned to the output limit without writing, and a prompt cache set too small by the operator. According to the operator's notes (not in a saved log), the engine does not take a thinking budget, so Tenaz could only lower the reasoning effort, which did not shorten the replies. Tenaz main 36f2131 includes the change that stops processes verify leaves behind; no orphaned servers were found.
