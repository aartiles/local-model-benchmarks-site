25 October 2026 · Agentic game · 3D western action

Wild West Showdown 3D · Sushi 1.2.4

Session completed Local run

Tenaz reached COMPLETE after 104 h 10 min (101 h 8 min excluding pauses and one block): a playable western town with seven rivals, DRAW duels, money, reputation, a store, a final boss and a champion screen. 86 tasks done, 14 split and 3 dropped over 575 sessions; the project's verify passes 126 checks and the second final review accepted all 73 requirements, 7 of them by inspection. A real-timing playtest beat all seven rivals. In the boss duel the player's own model hides the boss and a narration line sits over the reticle.

What was checked

  • Bootstrap was accepted on its first session: 73 requirements and 25 task cards. 17 replans and 15 splits were accepted; one replan was rejected for leaving four requirements uncovered.
  • Each task was accepted only after verify --all and the task's own checks passed and a fresh reviewer returned ACCEPT; reviewers returned CHANGES 38 times. Three tasks were dropped: one whose work had already landed, one that kept failing on the virtual clock, and one asking for a real multi-touch proof that synthetic pointers cannot give.
  • The first final review returned CHANGES and added seven tasks; the second accepted, with R12, R14, R20, R23, R31, R71 and R73 marked as manual (judged by playing or looking).
  • Of 575 sessions, 276 ended early: 211 at the context budget (Tenaz continued them in fresh sessions), 30 at a turn or no-progress watchdog, 16 on an engine error, 16 when fx exited mid-turn (14 of them with 'stream did not contain valid UTF-8' after a shell command's output cut a multibyte character in half), and 3 at the operator's pauses.
  • On 24 October at 07:28 UTC Tenaz blocked with 'engine unavailable' after seven sessions in a row ended that way; Sushi was answering throughout, and the cause was the fx crash above, repeated by greedy sampling. The run resumed at 10:26 UTC on a build that charges such a crash to the task; it did so three more times (T030a and T031 later passed; T030b was split).
  • Independent check after the run: verify --all passed 126/126 in 51 s. A real-timing playtest in system Chrome, with the test hooks used only to read state and to stand next to each rival, found no console errors in normal mode, a 'TOO EARLY!' loss for a false start and a 'TOO SLOW!' loss for no shot, reputation gates at 20, 45 and 120, all seven rivals beaten with 180–220 ms reactions, and a WILD WEST CHAMPION screen with time, duels, fastest draw and money.
  • Seen in the playtest captures: in the final duel the player's model hides Jeremiah Cade and the line 'The tower is at his back. Steady.' covers the reticle at DRAW; dust motes are large and dense. Not checked independently: a store purchase, PLAY AGAIN, audio, and touch on a real iPad.

Limits of this evidence

Not a clean single-version run: four Tenaz builds, fixed during the run for defects it exposed (a context budget that never rose again, shell calls without an action field, invented absolute paths, and fx crashes counted as engine failures), and one protocol refresh. The machine was shared with other memory-heavy applications for much of the run, so its wall time says more about those conditions than about the model. The published build is the run's final commit, unchanged; the 2026-10-10 TensorFold attempt of this challenge used a different runner, harness, model and engine, so the two runs are described side by side, not compared as a controlled pair.
