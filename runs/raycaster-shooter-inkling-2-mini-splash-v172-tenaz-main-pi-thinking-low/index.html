What was checked

  • Runner final state COMPLETE on 24 November 09:07:08.441 UTC: 11 tasks done, none pending. Bootstrap passed its three steps first time (16 requirements, R16 manual; 11 tasks). Every task passed verify and review on its first attempt (11 of 11 ACCEPT). Two replans added nothing. Final review cycle 1 returned ACCEPT with verify passing and all 16 requirements covered, R16 by hand.
  • Runner events: 28 sessions, none stopped or failed; no watchdog actions, reverts, context compactions, provider errors, capacity limits or engine retries. No reply stopped at the output limit: the stop reasons are 213 tool use and 28 stop, and the largest reply is 6,480 tokens of the 16,384 allowed.
  • Configuration: Pi 1.6.0 over RPC with the openai-completions provider splash, thinking low, text input only, a 131,072-token context window and max_tokens 16,384; Tenaz main 3f43405 with defaults, temperature 1.0, generic engine, sandbox off. The preflight reported reasoning, cache and tools true and vision not tested.
  • Discarded first launch: at 08:17 UTC the same setup with vision failed Tenaz's image preflight. Splash answered the image request with 400 (the model is served without vision; Splash 1.7.2's families.py marks Inkling 2 Mini's vision tower as not served yet). Tenaz stopped before any session. Its 5 preflight requests and the error are the first entries in the Splash log and are not part of this record.
  • `verify --all` on a clean clone of the final commit 17eb777 with the run's node_modules: 16 of 16 passed, as Tenaz recorded. Model-written checks, not an independent acceptance suite.
  • Played by Claude with Playwright in Chromium's new headless mode on 24 November, with real keyboard and mouse input against the archived build, plus an earlier headless play with a fake clock. Space at the spawn (4.5 cells from the door's centre) does nothing; after walking forward it slides the door open. The one enemy chases once it sees the player. A click lowers the ammo and draws the muzzle flash (caught on a frame in the fake-clock play), a hit enemy flashes white, and three hits fired before the enemy arrives kill it; it lies down and stays down. The game ran at about 60 fps there.
  • Faults found in play and in the source: the enemy has no stopping distance and walks onto the player's exact position, where shoot() skips any enemy closer than 0.05 cells. Once there it cannot be hit: in one play it stayed at 3 HP after eight shots. It never damages the player either (health stays 100). The map is 32 by 24 with one door, one enemy, one barrel and two pillars; the two left rooms (rows 1-9 and 15-22) are closed by full walls and cannot be reached, and one pillar is inside one of them. Walls shorter than the screen show their texture squeezed into a band across the middle (raycast.js clamps the texture rows). Enemies are simple geometric shapes and the ceiling reads as flat grey. The barrel in the spawn view is hidden behind the wall edge.
  • Mouse look: the user reports it does not work in their browser. In Chromium the click took pointer lock (document.pointerLockElement was the canvas) and pointerlockchange reached a listener on document, but not the game's bubble-phase listener on window, so the game's locked flag stayed false and mouse movement did not turn the view. Not checked in another browser.
  • Engine metrics: one Splash process wrote 250 Done summaries. The first 5 belong to the discarded launch, the next 4 to this run's preflight; all 241 proxy records match the remaining 241 one-to-one, in order and uniquely by (prompt, output, cached), ending at the last summary in the log. For all 28 sessions the matched summaries' fresh input, cached and output tokens equal the usage Tenaz journaled. TTFT comes from Splash's 'TTFT of request' lines.
  • Memory: Tenaz's 116 host samples at 30 s report memory pressure normal throughout. Wired memory went from 21.8 GiB at the first sample to a maximum of 42.3 GiB. Tenaz sampled Splash's Python parent (0.23 to 0.27 GiB), not its engine, so no engine footprint is recorded.
  • Public artifact: `vite build --base ./` of the final commit 17eb777 in the clean clone; its JavaScript bundle is byte-identical to the run's own dist, and only the asset path in index.html differs. Plain canvas code with no URLs in the bundle; it made no request outside its folder and logged no page errors in the headless play. Screenshot: a frame from that play at 1280x720, about 1 s after opening the door.

Limits of this evidence

Completion is the runner outcome. Inkling 2 Mini, a looped mixture of experts, as Unsloth's UD-Q4_K_M GGUF on Splash 1.7.2 with the DFlash2 drafter at a 128K context, Pi thinking low and text only, on the Raycaster brief of the Qwen 3.8 27B pi-adapted runs and of the Inkling 2 Mini mlx-serve run. Against that mlx-serve run on the same Tenaz build, this run changed the engine, the quantization (4-bit GGUF against the 8-bit MLX pack), the thinking (low against high) and vision (off against on) at once, so it is not a controlled comparison. The playthroughs were by Claude in headless Chromium, and by the user, who reported that mouse look does not work.
