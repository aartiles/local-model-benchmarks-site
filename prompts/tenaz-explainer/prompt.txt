# Tenaz Explainer

Make one 45-second silent explainer video about Tenaz with HyperFrames, and
make every picture in it yourself with the `generate_image` tool. You write
the script, the storyboard and the design; the facts are below. Use the
HyperFrames skills: read-only copies are in `.tenaz/skills/`. Read
`hyperframes-core/SKILL.md` before planning, `hyperframes-cli/SKILL.md`
before running any command, and `hyperframes-creative/SKILL.md` before
designing a scene. Read their `references/` files only for the step you are
working on, and read large files by section, not whole.

## Scope

- **One composition, rendered to `renders/explainer.mp4`.** 1920×1080
  landscape, 30 fps, exactly 45 s, with no audio track. The video is silent:
  everything it says is text on screen.
- **No questions and no approvals.** Nobody will answer. This brief is the
  approved brief and the final render is approved in advance: render when the
  checks pass. Every creative decision inside these rules is yours.
- **Offline.** There is no network. The HyperFrames CLI is installed in the
  project: run it as `./hf <command>` (for example `./hf lint`, `./hf check`,
  `./hf render`), never `npx`. Do not use `usage`, `catalog`, `add`, `tts`,
  `transcribe`, `auth`, `publish`, `cloud`, `lambda` or `cloudrun`: they need
  the network or an account.
- **Local files only.** GSAP is in `vendor/gsap.min.js`; load it with a
  relative path. Space Grotesk and JetBrains Mono are in `vendor/fonts/` with
  `@font-face` rules in `vendor/fonts/fonts.css`. Use no other fonts, no CDN
  and no remote URL. Do not change `vendor/`, `package.json` or
  `node_modules/`.
- **Inspect your own output.** Use `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` to read renders and
  to extract stills into `.agent/private/`, and look at them. Read only PNG or
  JPEG images, and combine several stills into one contact sheet rather than
  reading many.

## What Tenaz is (the facts)

Every claim in the video must come from this list. Do not add numbers,
features, benchmark results, customers, prices or comparisons that are not
here, and show no web address.

- Tenaz is a command-line tool. You give it a goal; it plans the work, splits
  it into tasks and drives a coding agent session by session until the goal
  is met, on a local model.
- It is built for machines that fit one model at a time: a laptop or a
  single GPU running a local LLM.
- Long agent runs on that hardware fail in predictable ways: the context
  fills up, the engine runs out of memory or restarts, the model loops on the
  same tool call, and an image model cannot be loaded next to the LLM. Tenaz
  handles those failures instead of counting them against the work.
- **The loop.** The goal is a short brief. The bootstrap turns it into
  numbered requirements, a `verify` script and a list of small tasks. Each
  task gets its own session: the agent builds it, Tenaz runs `verify`, and a
  fresh session of the same model reviews the change. A task that passes is
  committed; one that fails is undone and retried, split into smaller tasks or
  marked blocked. When every task is done, a final review checks the whole
  goal against the brief.
- **`verify` decides when the work is done.** The model saying it is done
  counts for nothing; only `verify` passing does. Tenaz runs it itself.
- **Fresh sessions, state on disk.** Every unit of work is a new agent
  session with a short context. The plan, task cards, notes and progress live
  in the project, and every step is a git commit.
- **The runner decides, not the model:** whether to commit or revert, and
  when to retry, split a task, block or replan.
- **Engine failures are not task failures.** A crash or a refused connection
  is retried with backoff without charging the task.
- **Watchdogs.** Loops, repeated calls, idle turns and huge outputs get a
  steering message first; then the session is stopped.
- **One resident model.** When the agent asks for an image, Tenaz unloads the
  LLM, runs the image model, frees it and reloads the LLM. The agent's
  requests wait meanwhile instead of failing.
- **Pause and resume.** Ctrl-C pauses after the current session; running it
  again continues.
- A frontier agent such as Claude Code or Codex can hand Tenaz a long goal
  and check the result when it is done.
- The commands: `tenaz init` creates a run from a brief, `tenaz run` works
  until the goal is met, `tenaz status` shows where it is, and `tenaz demo`
  shows a whole scripted run in about a minute with no model.
- **This video** was planned, illustrated and edited by a local model driven
  by Tenaz: Qwen 3.8 Flash Next on Sushi writing the code, and FLUX 3 Image
  Klein in ComfyUI making the pictures, on one MacBook Pro with 64 GB, with
  no one stepping in.

## The story

Six to eight scenes, in this order. The words are yours, except where a text
is given in quotes, which is exact.

1. **Hook:** what Tenaz is, in one line.
2. **The problem:** one machine that fits one model, and how long runs on it
   fail.
3. **The loop:** an animated diagram whose steps are labelled exactly
   "Bootstrap", "Build", "Verify", "Review" and "Commit", in that order, with
   the path back for a retry. The steps light up one after another as a task
   goes through them.
4. **Verify decides:** the model's claim against the check that runs.
5. **One resident model:** the LLM steps out, the image model makes a
   picture, the LLM comes back. Show one of your own generated pictures being
   made here.
6. **What keeps it going:** at least two of fresh sessions with state on disk,
   git commits, watchdogs, engine failures retried, pause and resume.
7. **End card:** "Tenaz", the command "tenaz demo", and the line "Planned,
   illustrated and edited by a local model with Tenaz." Everything still for
   the last 2 s.

A scene may also show a terminal with the real commands above, or a short
`tenaz init` and `tenaz run` exchange you write from the facts.

## Pictures

- **Only `generate_image`** makes raster images. Every PNG or JPEG in the
  video comes from a `generate_image` call and is copied, unchanged, from the
  path the call returns into `assets/images/`. Nothing downloaded, no
  placeholder files, no screenshots of the composition used as pictures.
- **Diagrams, text, terminals, arrows and icons** are drawn in the
  composition with HTML, CSS and inline SVG, not generated. They must not
  imitate a photograph.
- **At most 10 `generate_image` calls for the whole project**, retries
  included. Plan the prompts before the first call. FLUX writes text badly:
  ask for no letters, words, logos, screens with text or watermarks
  (`negative_prompt` helps), and put all text in HTML.
- **Fixed slots, size and seeds.** Five or six pictures, all 1536×864 (the
  video scales them up), with these seeds. A retry keeps the slot's seed and
  changes only the prompt.

  | Slot | File | Seed |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | `assets/images/image-1.png` | 101 |
  | 2 | `assets/images/image-2.png` | 102 |
  | 3 | `assets/images/image-3.png` | 103 |
  | 4 | `assets/images/image-4.png` | 104 |
  | 5 | `assets/images/image-5.png` | 105 |
  | 6 (optional) | `assets/images/image-6.png` | 106 |

- **One visual family:** the pictures share one palette, lighting and style,
  and that style matches the composition's design. Earlier runs with this
  image model drifted from picture to picture, so write one style sentence
  (medium, palette, light, lens) in `DESIGN.md` and use it word for word in
  every prompt, and keep each subject simple: one object or scene, not a
  story in one picture.
- **Look at every picture before you use it.** Open each returned file and
  compare it with the others in one contact sheet. Retry a picture that has
  text, a logo, a screen with text, a garbled object, or a style that does
  not match; record why in `DECISIONS.md`.
- **Pictures are slow and swap the models.** Each call takes one to three
  minutes, and the LLM is unloaded and reloaded around it. Make the pictures
  together, early, in one task, and do not start the next call before the
  last one has returned. A call that fails without making a picture still
  counts against the budget; keep one or two calls in reserve for retries.
- **Keep `assets/images/manifest.json`** current. It lists every
  `generate_image` call in order, including those whose result you did not
  use: `{"calls": [{"n", "slot", "prompt", "negative_prompt", "seed",
  "width", "height", "returned": [paths], "used_as": "assets/images/<file>" |
  null}]}`.

## Design

- **A short technical film, not a slide deck.** The explanation is carried by
  motion: things move, connect, light up and transform. Each scene enters
  with one clear move, never a plain cut, and its elements stagger in.
  Earlier videos by local models read like animated slides: a picture, a
  headline and a fade, scene after scene. Avoid that: show the system
  working (a task card travelling through the loop, a check turning from red
  to green, the LLM and the image model trading places in memory), let
  elements carry over from one scene to the next, and keep something moving
  in every second of the video except the final hold. Pictures move too:
  slow pushes, pans or parallax, not still backdrops.
- **One look, chosen by you** and written in `DESIGN.md` before the first
  scene: at most five colours with their hex values, how the two fonts are
  used, how pictures are framed, and the motion rules (eases and durations).
- **Readable without sound.** Headlines 88 px or larger, other text 40 px or
  larger, and labels in JetBrains Mono 32 px or larger. Every text stays on
  screen for at least 2 s plus 0.25 s per word, and no frame shows more than
  25 words. Text over a picture sits on a plate or a darkened area so it is
  readable.
- **Safe area:** all text inside the central 90% of the frame.

## Checks `verify` must run

Within 300 s:

- `./hf lint` and `./hf check`, and require both to pass.
- Every file in `assets/images/` other than `manifest.json` is a valid PNG of
  1536×864, not flat (standard deviation of its luminance above 12 on 0–255),
  and no two files have the same SHA-256.
- Every picture's SHA-256 equals that of the `returned` path that
  `manifest.json` names for it. When that path no longer exists (the project
  was copied elsewhere), this one check reports skipped, not passed.
- `manifest.json` has at most 10 calls, and each used call's seed and size
  match its slot.
- The composition uses every picture in `assets/images/`, and loads nothing
  outside the project.
- A draft render: size, frame rate, duration and no audio stream, checked
  with `ffprobe`.
- Stills at the middle of each scene into `.agent/private/frames/`, combined
  into one contact sheet, with the stills and the sheet listed as artifacts,
  so that a reviewer can compare the scenes with `DESIGN.md` and the story.

The final task renders `renders/explainer.mp4` at normal quality (outside
`verify`'s time limit if needed, with the CLI's detached render) and checks it
the same way.

## Done means

`renders/explainer.mp4` tells the story above in 45 s, every picture in it was
made by `generate_image`, every claim is in the facts, and `PLAN.md`,
`STATE.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `DECISIONS.md` exist and reflect the finished
project, including why each picture was kept or retried.
