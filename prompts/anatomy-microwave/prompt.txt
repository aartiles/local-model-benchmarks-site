# Microwave Oven Anatomy

Build one interactive isometric figure that explains how a microwave oven
heats food. Use the `anatomy` skill: it is loaded in every session, and a
read-only copy is in `.agent/skills/anatomy/`. Read its `SKILL.md` before
planning. Read its `references/` files only for the step you are working on,
and read large files by section, not whole.

## Scope

- **Size: Figure.** One mechanism, 40–80 parts, SVG with at most one WebGL
  layer, no 3D mode. Do not ask anyone to choose a size or confirm a plan; every
  decision is yours.
- **Framework-free.** Use a Node build script (`build.mjs` at the project root)
  that imports the kit and writes a standalone page, following the skill's
  standalone path. There is no `npm install` and no network access.
- **Project-local kit.** Copy what you need from `.agent/skills/anatomy/kit/` and
  `scripts/` into the project. Never build or write inside `.agent/skills/`; it is
  read-only. To study an example, copy its folder into `.agent/private/` and
  build it there.

## The idea to explain (truth first)

A microwave oven turns mains electricity into radio waves that shake water
molecules in the food. The parts, in the order the energy flows:

1. A **high-voltage transformer** raises the mains voltage to about 2 kV AC.
   A **capacitor and diode** form a voltage doubler that drives the magnetron
   at about 4 kV.
2. The **magnetron**: a heated cathode at the centre of a copper anode block
   with resonant cavities. Two ring magnets make the electrons spiral past the
   cavities, which ring at 2.45 GHz. A short antenna couples the waves out. A
   fan cools it.
3. A **waveguide**, a rectangular metal duct, carries the waves into the
   cooking cavity.
4. The **cavity** is a metal box, so the waves reflect and form a standing
   wave. At 2.45 GHz the wavelength is about 12.2 cm, so hot spots repeat about
   every 6.1 cm (half a wavelength), with cold spots between them.
5. The **food**: the field reverses 2.45 billion times a second and keeps
   twisting the water molecules, which heats the water. The **turntable**
   (about 5 rpm, one turn in about 12 s) carries the food through hot and cold
   spots so it heats more evenly.
6. The **door** has a metal mesh with holes of about 1 mm. They are far
   smaller than the 12 cm wave, so the waves stay in while light gets out.
   **Interlock switches** cut the magnetron the instant the door opens.

Numbers for the model (typical values; the caption must call them typical):

- 800 W of microwave output at full power, from about 1200 W drawn from the
  mains.
- Lower power levels do not weaken the magnetron. It switches fully on and off
  over a cycle of about 30 s, so 50% power means 15 s on and 15 s off.
- Liquid water: heating needs 4.18 J per gram per °C. Assume a cup of liquid
  water absorbs the full output. 250 g of water at 20 °C reaches 100 °C in about
  105 s at full power.
- Ice absorbs microwaves far less than liquid water. Assume 10% of the water
  rate while frozen, and say in the caption that this is the figure's
  assumption. Melting takes 334 J per gram, during which the temperature stays
  at 0 °C.
- An empty ceramic dish absorbs almost nothing.

The figure's readout, motion and caption must follow from a small pure model
module (`model.mjs`) that implements this behaviour with these numbers. Every
formula has an edge case, such as zero time, the door open, or the magnetron
off part of a cycle. Test each one.

## The object

The object is a real countertop microwave oven, drawn as a cutaway: the outer
case opened so the reader sees the magnetron and its high-voltage parts, the
waveguide, the cavity with its turntable, and the door. Draw it the way a real
one is built: screws, vents, the mesh door, the control panel, and cables with
connectors. Say in one sentence in `.agent/spec.md` what moves and what that
motion means.

## Interaction

- The reader can choose at least: the power level (100%, 50%, 30%); what is
  inside (a cup of water, a frozen block, an empty dish); the turntable on or
  off; and the door open or closed.
- An idle tour shows these on its own when nobody interacts.
- Keyboard and pointer both work.
- Motion stops when the figure is offscreen and goes still under reduced
  motion.
- The live readout shows at least: power level and whether the magnetron is
  on right now, elapsed time, energy delivered, and the food's temperature or
  its melted share.

## Deliverable

- `node build.mjs` writes `dist/index.html` (dark), and `node build.mjs --light`
  writes `dist/index-light.html`. Both are standalone pages that open from the
  file system.
- The figure sits in the skill's framed card with a title, hint, live readout,
  and a true caption under the card.

## Checks

`./verify --all` must finish within 240 seconds. It covers:

- the model module's behaviour: heating of water by the energy balance;
  duty-cycle power levels; no power with the door open; the melting plateau at
  0 °C; the 10% ice rate; hot-spot spacing of half a wavelength; and the edge
  cases above;
- both pages build, and load in headless Chrome without console errors (the
  skill's `scripts/capture.mjs`);
- `node build.mjs --audit` passes whenever the figure has pipes, cables or
  round parts;
- the skill's line-end check (`scripts/lines.mjs`) reports `line ends: 0` on
  both pages;
- screenshots of the dark and light pages at desktop width and at 390 px, plus
  one taken mid-motion. They are written under `.agent/private/shots/` and
  listed in the verify JSON's `artifacts`.

Look at the screenshots yourself with the read tool, and judge them against the
skill's quality bar. A blank or broken image is a failure, even when every
check passes.

## Done means

A reader who opens `dist/index.html` recognises a detailed microwave oven.
They can see the energy travel from the transformer through the magnetron and
waveguide into the cavity. The food heats as the model says. Lower power
levels visibly cycle the magnetron on and off, and opening the door stops it.
The readout numbers follow from the model, and every check passes.
