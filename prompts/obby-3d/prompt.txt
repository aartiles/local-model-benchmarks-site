# Obby 3D

Build a complete, polished and playable 3D obstacle-course game inspired by classic platforming obbies.

The game must run in a modern web browser and must be fully playable on both desktop and touch devices, with iPad/tablet as a first-class target.

Use Three.js for rendering. You may use additional lightweight libraries if useful, but the final project must be straightforward to run locally.

The objective is to reach the top of a colorful floating obstacle course consisting of 10 distinct stages.

## Core gameplay

Create a third-person player character that can:

- Walk and run across platforms.
- Jump.
- Fall under gravity.
- Land reliably on platforms.
- Die when touching hazards or falling out of the level.
- Respawn at the most recently activated checkpoint.

Movement should feel responsive and arcade-like rather than physically realistic.

The player must never be able to fly, double-jump or climb vertically unless a stage explicitly provides a mechanism for doing so.

## Controls

### Desktop

- WASD or arrow keys: move.
- Space: jump.
- Mouse drag or pointer lock: rotate the camera.

### Touch / tablet

The entire game must be completable without a physical keyboard or mouse.

Provide:

- A virtual joystick on the lower-left side for movement.
- A large jump button on the lower-right side.
- Dragging on the right side of the screen rotates the camera.
- Movement and camera control must work simultaneously using multi-touch.
- The jump button must remain usable while another finger is controlling movement or camera.

Touching the game must not accidentally scroll, zoom or select the surrounding page.

Controls must be large enough to use comfortably on an iPad.

The primary mobile target is tablet landscape orientation, although the interface should remain usable at other reasonable viewport sizes.

## Camera

Use a third-person follow camera positioned behind and above the player.

The camera must:

- Follow the player smoothly.
- Rotate horizontally around the player.
- Allow limited vertical rotation.
- Avoid flipping upside down.
- Keep the player visible during normal gameplay.
- Behave sensibly after respawning.

Do not require extremely precise camera positioning to complete jumps.

## Course

The course must contain exactly 10 numbered stages arranged as a visually coherent ascending obstacle course.

Each stage should introduce or combine a meaningful gameplay idea.

Use approximately this progression:

1. Basic platform jumps.
2. Longer jumps and platforms at different heights.
3. Narrow beams or paths requiring careful movement.
4. Hazard blocks or lava that kill the player on contact.
5. Moving platforms.
6. Rotating obstacle bars or sweepers.
7. Platforms that periodically disappear and return.
8. Conveyor platforms that push the player.
9. A vertical sequence combining moving platforms and jumps.
10. A final gauntlet combining several previously introduced mechanics.

You may adapt the exact geometry and visual design, but all ten gameplay concepts must be represented.

## Physical consistency

Every required jump and obstacle MUST be achievable using the actual movement speed, jump velocity and gravity implemented in the game.

Design the course using the real movement capabilities of the player.

Do not place required platforms beyond the player's maximum achievable jump distance or height.

The player must be able to complete the entire course without:

- exploiting collision bugs,
- jumping outside the intended route,
- frame-perfect input,
- pixel-perfect positioning,
- unusually precise camera angles,
- or impossible jumps.

The normal intended route from Stage 1 through Stage 10 must be physically completable.

## Moving platforms

Moving platforms must interact correctly with the player.

When standing on a moving platform, the player should be carried along by it instead of remaining stationary in world space and sliding off.

Moving platforms must not pass unpredictably through the player or make required jumps impossible.

## Rotating obstacles

At least one stage must contain a rotating bar or sweeper.

Touching the dangerous part should either kill the player or clearly knock the player from the platform.

Its speed must allow a reasonably skilled player to pass it.

## Disappearing platforms

At least one stage must contain platforms that alternate between active and inactive states.

Their timing must be readable and fair.

Inactive platforms must genuinely stop supporting the player.

They must later return so the stage can always be retried.

## Conveyors

At least one stage must contain conveyor platforms.

Standing on them should visibly push the player in a defined direction while still allowing the player to move and jump.

## Hazards and falling

Clearly distinguish dangerous surfaces from safe platforms.

For example:

- red/orange glowing surfaces may represent hazards,
- neutral or brightly colored blocks may be safe.

Touching a hazard immediately kills the player.

Falling sufficiently below the course also kills the player.

Death should trigger a quick respawn rather than requiring a page reload.

## Checkpoints

Include checkpoints throughout the course, at minimum after Stages:

- 2
- 4
- 6
- 8

and before or at the start of the final stage.

A checkpoint must activate when the player reaches it.

Once activated, subsequent deaths must respawn the player at that checkpoint rather than at the beginning.

Give clear visual feedback when a new checkpoint activates.

Respawning must place the player safely above or on the checkpoint and must reset vertical velocity and any invalid movement state.

## Finish

Stage 10 must end with a clearly identifiable finish platform, portal, flag or similar goal.

Reaching it completes the game.

Display a completion screen showing:

- OBBY COMPLETE
- completion time
- total deaths
- a button to play again

Restarting should reset the timer, deaths and checkpoint progress.

## HUD

During gameplay display a simple unobtrusive HUD containing:

- Stage X / 10
- elapsed time
- deaths

The current stage must update as the player progresses.

On touch devices, the HUD must not overlap the joystick, jump button or important areas of the screen.

## Visual style

Create an original colorful 3D obstacle-course aesthetic.

Aim for something playful, readable and visually attractive, using:

- floating platforms,
- saturated colors,
- a bright sky,
- clouds or distant environmental elements,
- clear height progression,
- simple geometric decoration,
- subtle shadows,
- lightweight particles or effects where appropriate.

Do not reproduce Roblox branding, characters, UI or copyrighted assets.

The player may be a simple stylized character built from primitive geometry.

External 3D models are not necessary.

Gameplay readability is more important than visual complexity.

## Level readability

The player should generally understand where to go next.

Use platform placement, elevation, color, checkpoints and environmental composition to guide progression.

Avoid large ambiguous areas or routes where it is unclear which platform belongs to the next stage.

Stages should feel visually distinct enough that progression is obvious.

## Performance

The game should run smoothly in a modern desktop browser and on a reasonably recent iPad.

Avoid unnecessarily expensive geometry, lighting, post-processing or physics.

Use responsive sizing and adapt correctly when the viewport changes.

## Robustness

Pay particular attention to:

- grounded detection,
- collision handling,
- landing on platform edges,
- jumping underneath geometry,
- moving-platform interaction,
- simultaneous touch inputs,
- camera controls,
- death and respawn,
- checkpoint persistence,
- finishing and restarting.

The game should remain playable after repeatedly dying and respawning.

Do not allow the player to become permanently stuck because of an invalid physics state.

## Starting experience

When the game loads, show a short start screen with:

OBBY 3D

Reach the top.

Desktop:
WASD + Space + Mouse

Touch:
Joystick + Jump + Drag to look

START

On touch devices, begin gameplay directly from the START interaction so that browser input restrictions are handled correctly.

## Required outcome

Deliver a complete game, not a tech demo.

A successful implementation must allow a player to:

1. Start the game.
2. Control the character comfortably.
3. Rotate the camera.
4. Complete all ten stages in order.
5. Activate checkpoints.
6. Die and correctly respawn.
7. Interact correctly with moving, rotating, disappearing and conveyor obstacles.
8. Reach Stage 10.
9. Finish the course.
10. See their completion time and death count.
11. Restart and play again.

Most importantly, test the gameplay logic against the movement constants you implement: the intended course must actually be completable from beginning to end.
