Task requirements & full brief

Tenaz unloads the language model through Sushi's API before each image, runs FLUX 3 Image Klein in ComfyUI and loads the language model again. Renders are scored with hidden acceptance cases read from the video (format, silence, labels and their order by OCR, the end card and its hold, words per frame, reading time, safe area) and from the project (picture slots, provenance against the image tool's output, the call budget against Tenaz's journal). Taste and whether the video explains Tenaz are judged by eye. No transcripts are published.

  • Seven beats in 45 s at 1920&times;1080 and 30 fps: hook, problem, the loop, verify decides, one resident model, what keeps it going, end card.
  • Exact texts for the loop's five labels and the end card; every other claim from a fact sheet taken from Tenaz's README.
  • Every picture from the runner's generate_image tool, at most 10 calls, with fixed slots, sizes and seeds and one style sentence in every prompt.
  • Readable without sound: type sizes, reading time, at most 25 words per frame, a safe area.
  • A technical film rather than a slide deck: motion in every second, elements carried between scenes, pictures that move.

How to read this comparison

Compare the generated builds and the checks recorded for each attempt. There is no normalised quality score, and the cloud task’s cumulative token totals and end-to-end rate include repeated context, reasoning, tool calls and waiting — they are not comparable with local per-request decode or prefill rates. The method page sets out which figures can and cannot be set against each other.

Attempts run with Tenaz used the open-source runner at github.com/aartiles/tenaz. It plans the brief into tasks, builds each in a fresh agent session and accepts it only when its checks pass and a reviewer agrees. The method page describes it and the runners before it.
